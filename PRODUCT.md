## Cover-first title evidence policy

For each paginated document, title recognition reads at most pages 1–2, with explicit first-page titles preferred and multiple title candidates selectable. Failure to find a title never expands the reading budget. PDF uses physical pages and OCR only within that opening; DOCX with saved rendered page markers stops before page 3. Explicit title/project-field labels are evidence labels, not part of the proposed filename; contiguous prominent cover-title lines may be joined as a complete title before individual fragments.

The user explicitly allows a bounded opening excerpt when Word has no reliable pagination. DOCX without rendered page markers uses at most 300 characters; legacy DOC/RTF conversion reads only a bounded opening through textutil. Evidence is labelled Word 开头节选, never represented as two physical pages. Naming excerpts remain separate from archive full-text caching.

XLSX recognition uses horizontal and vertical headers only. Ordinary title candidates come from the first 8 rows and 16 columns. The first 3 columns may extend through row 30 solely to join adjacent single-character vertical headers; large or bold body text below row 8 is not a standalone title source. Hidden sheets are excluded, shared strings are resolved only for the retained header cells, and formulas are not executed. Legacy XLS is not parsed for naming and retains its original name.

Without a reliable title, preserve the exact original filename, including its extension, even when a prefix format is configured. Confirming an untouched fallback closes the single-item dialog without a filesystem rename or rename record; manual edits remain confirmable. Batch items without titles start excluded and unchanged, and can be edited then included. Generating or editing candidates never renames files; explicit confirmation remains mandatory. Existing identity/version/root/collision guards remain in force.

This title-policy change preserves the incumbent visual system, current 20261031(02) version and explicit saved naming-format choices. It is an ordinary functional extension, not a design-system refresh.
